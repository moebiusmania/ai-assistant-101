Online you’ll find thousands of “tricks” and “magic prompts”. The problem? Assistants change fast, and what worked six months ago may be useless today.

That’s why this course is built on a sturdier idea: **<Term id="fluency">AI Fluency</Term>**, the ability to work with AI in a way that is:

- **effective** → you get what you really need;
- **efficient** → without losing more time than you save;
- **ethical** → honest, towards others and yourself;
- **safe** → protecting your data and other people’s.

<Callout type="info">
  <p>This lesson is inspired by the <strong>AI Fluency Framework</strong> by Rick Dakan and Joseph Feller, also used in Anthropic Academy courses. Here it’s explained simply, with everyday examples.</p>
</Callout>

## Think of AI as a collaborator

Imagine a new colleague: super fast, available at any hour, has read everything… but **doesn’t know you**, knows nothing about your situation and now and then says wrong things with great confidence.

To work well with them you need four skills. These are the **4Ds**:

The 4Ds aren’t steps in a strict order: they intertwine. But if you ask yourself their four questions, you’ll avoid almost all the common mistakes.

## Three ways of working with AI

Not all collaborations are the same. The framework describes three modes:

<div class="modes">

**🤖 Automation** — the AI carries out a specific task you gave it. *“Translate this email into Italian.”* You give the order, it executes.

**🤝 Augmentation** — you and the AI think together, like two colleagues. *“Help me figure out if this mortgage is right for me: ask me questions.”* The result comes from the back-and-forth.

**🧭 Agency** — you set up the AI to work **on its own on your behalf**, maybe talking to other people or using other programs. *“Create an assistant that answers my B&B guests’ frequent questions.”* Here diligence matters even more.

</div>

## Why it’s worth learning

People who use AI without a method usually end up at one of two extremes:

- **Blind trust**: they copy-paste everything, and sooner or later get burned by a mistake.
- **Total rejection**: after one disappointing answer they decide it’s “useless”.

The 4Ds keep you in the middle: you use AI where it’s strong, check it where it’s weak, and you stay in charge.
