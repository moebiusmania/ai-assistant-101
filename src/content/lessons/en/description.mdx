What you write to the assistant is called a <Term id="prompt">prompt</Term>. You don’t need a magic formula: you need to **explain yourself as you would to a capable person who doesn’t know you**.

You don’t need all of them every time. But the more important the task, the more ingredients are worth adding.

<Callout type="key" title="The three things to describe">
  <p>The 4D framework splits Description into three parts:</p>
  <p><strong>Product</strong> — what you want: format, length, tone, audience.<br />
  <strong>Process</strong> — how it should work on it: “ask me questions first”, “think step by step”, “give me 3 alternatives”.<br />
  <strong>Performance</strong> — what kind of partner you want: “be critical and direct”, “explain it as to a beginner”.</p>
</Callout>

## 5 habits that make the difference

### 1. Give examples

If you have an example of what you like, show it. *“Write a caption for this photo in the same style as these two I’ve already posted: [example 1], [example 2].”*

### 2. Ask it to ask you questions

One of the most useful requests of all: **“Before answering, ask me the questions you need.”** The AI will help you clarify things you hadn’t thought of.

### 3. Break big tasks down

Instead of “organise my house move”, go in stages: first the to-do list, then the schedule, then the messages to send.

### 4. Don’t settle for the first answer

A chat is a conversation: you can always say *“shorter”*, *“less formal”*, *“point 3 doesn’t convince me, suggest something else”*. No need to start over.

### 5. Write the way you talk

You don’t need “computer language”. Normal, complete sentences work great. Voice works too: many assistants let you dictate.

## A formula to remember

If you want a simple reminder, think of these four questions:

> **Who I am · What I want · How I want it · What to avoid**
