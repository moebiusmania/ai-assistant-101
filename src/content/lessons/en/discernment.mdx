AI writes well. **Too well**: even when it’s wrong, the text is tidy, confident, convincing. Discernment is the ability not to be charmed by the form and to look at the substance.

## Three things to evaluate

The 4D framework suggests looking at an answer from three angles:

1. **The product** — is it correct? Complete? Right for my purpose and audience?
2. **The process** — do the steps make sense? Any logical leaps or rushed conclusions?
3. **The performance** — did it follow my instructions? Is it just agreeing with me?

<Callout type="warn" title="Watch out for the AI that “agrees with you”">
  <p>Assistants tend to be accommodating. If you ask “my idea is brilliant, right?”, they’ll probably say yes. For an honest opinion, ask explicitly: <em>“Tell me the 3 weak points of this idea.”</em></p>
</Callout>

## The warning signs

These are the signals that should make your antennae go up:

- **Very precise numbers, dates and percentages** without a source.
- **Quotes** attributed to famous people.
- **Titles of books, studies, laws** or specific court rulings.
- **Links** and web addresses (often made up or broken).
- **Absolute promises**: “always”, “never”, “guaranteed”, “risk-free”.
- **Recent** news, if the assistant didn’t search the web.

## The Description ↔ Discernment loop

Describing and evaluating aren’t two separate moments: they form a **loop**. Ask → evaluate → adjust the request → evaluate again. That’s how you get to a good result.

## How to check (without going crazy)

You don’t need to check everything. Use common sense and the **risk level** from the Delegation lesson. When it matters, here’s how:

<Checklist
  id="discernment"
  title="Before using an important answer"
  items={[
    'Did I check numbers, dates and names against a reliable source?',
    'Did I open the links to see if they really exist?',
    'Did I ask the AI: “How sure are you? What could be wrong?”',
    'Did I ask for sources, and check them myself?',
    'Does the answer fit with what I already know?',
    'For health, money or law: did I talk to a professional?',
  ]}
/>

<Callout type="tip" title="The second-opinion trick">
  <p>Open a new chat (or another assistant) and ask it to <strong>review</strong> the answer: “Find any mistakes in this text”. It’s no guarantee, but it often brings weak spots to light.</p>
</Callout>

## Watch out for bias too

AI learned from texts written by people, and inherited their <Term id="bias">biases</Term> too. For example it might assume “the nurse” is a woman and “the engineer” a man, or always give examples from just one culture. If you notice it, point it out and ask for a more balanced version.
