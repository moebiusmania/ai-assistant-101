## 1. Delegation: split the work

Before you write anything, decide **who does what**. The AI is good at drafts, ideas and structure. Decisions, money and final checks stay with you.

<Track id="trip">

| The AI does | You do |
| --- | --- |
| Proposes a programme with timings | Choose dates, budget and who’s coming |
| Suggests places, activities or a menu | Check opening hours, prices and availability on official sites |
| Turns the plan into a checklist and a timeline | Book and pay, yourself |

</Track>

<Track id="meals">

| The AI does | You do |
| --- | --- |
| Proposes dinners that fit your constraints | Decide what you like and what you can cook |
| Writes the shopping list grouped by aisle | Check what’s already in the fridge and cupboard |
| Suggests how to reuse leftovers | Make allergy and health decisions with a qualified person |

</Track>

<Track id="skill">

| The AI does | You do |
| --- | --- |
| Breaks the skill into small daily steps | Choose the goal and how much time you have |
| Explains, sets exercises and corrects you | Do the exercises: that’s where the learning happens |
| Quizzes you on what you’ve learnt | Check important facts against a reliable source |

</Track>

## 4. Refine together

The first answer is a draft. Improve it with **short follow-ups**: copy these, adapt them and keep going until the result is really yours.

<Track id="trip">

- “Saturday afternoon is too packed: drop one activity and add a break.”
- “Give me a plan B in case it rains.”
- “Turn the plan into a checklist split into ‘two weeks before’, ‘the day before’ and ‘on the day’.”
- “Write a short message to invite the others, with the date, the time and what to bring.”

</Track>

<Track id="meals">

- “On Tuesday I get home late: swap it for something ready in 15 minutes.”
- “Now write the shopping list grouped by aisle, leaving out what I already have.”
- “How can I reuse Monday’s leftovers in another dinner?”
- “Make the list cheaper: what can I swap without changing the recipes too much?”

</Track>

<Track id="skill">

- “Day 3 was too hard: explain it with an everyday example and give me an easier exercise.”
- “Quiz me on what I’ve learnt so far, one question at a time, and wait for my answer.”
- “Here’s my exercise: [paste]. Don’t give me the solution: tell me where I went wrong.”
- “Make me a one-page summary of the week to keep.”

</Track>

<Callout type="info">
  <p>If the chat gets very long and the AI starts “forgetting” things, open a new one and start with a short summary of what you’ve decided so far.</p>
</Callout>

## 5. Diligence: before you use it

<Track id="trip">

- **Don’t paste** other people’s phone numbers, addresses or payment details into the chat. Use placeholders like [GUEST 1].
- **Book and pay** only on official sites you found yourself, not on links you haven’t checked.
- If someone has **allergies or special needs**, ask them directly: don’t rely on the AI’s guesses.

</Track>

<Track id="meals">

- You can mention general preferences (“no meat”, “not much salt”), but **avoid sharing detailed health information**.
- For allergies, intolerances or medical diets, the last word goes to **a doctor or a dietitian**.
- **Read the labels**: the AI doesn’t know the exact products you buy.

</Track>

<Track id="skill">

- If the skill involves **safety** (electricity, medicines, tools, driving), use the AI to understand, then follow the official instructions or a professional.
- If it’s for **school or work**, follow the rules on using AI and say how you used it.
- The goal is for **you** to learn: try each exercise before asking for the solution.

</Track>

<Callout type="key" title="The result is yours">
  <p>The AI suggested, you decided. If something goes wrong, the responsibility isn’t the assistant’s: that’s exactly why you checked.</p>
</Callout>
