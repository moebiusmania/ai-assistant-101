The last D may be the most important: **you are responsible** for how you use AI and for what comes out of it. The framework splits it into three moments.

## 1. When you choose (what to use and what to share)

### Protect your data

When you type in a chat, the text is sent to the servers of the company offering the service. Depending on the settings, conversations may be **stored**, **read by reviewers** or **used to improve the model**.

<Callout type="warn" title="Never type into a chat">
  <p>Passwords and access codes · full credit card numbers and bank account details · PINs and one-time codes · ID documents · your or other people’s health data without thinking · confidential work information.</p>
</Callout>

The good news: you can almost always **get the same help without personal data**. Just replace it with placeholders like [NAME] or [ADDRESS].

<Callout type="tip" title="Check your settings">
  <p>Almost every assistant has an option to <strong>not use your chats for training</strong> and a temporary-chat mode. Look under “Settings” → “Privacy” or “Data”. If you use AI for work, ask which tools your company has approved.</p>
</Callout>

## 2. When you share (transparency)

If you used AI in a significant way for a piece of work, **say so** when it matters: at school, university, in an article, in a work document. Nothing to be ashamed of: it’s a tool, like a calculator or spell-checker.

- At school and work, **check the rules**: some allow AI, some don’t, some only in certain ways.
- A simple formula: *“I used an AI assistant for the first draft and proofreading; the content and fact-checking are mine.”*
- **Don’t pass off as your own** a creative text written entirely by AI, if the person receiving it expects you wrote it.

## 3. When you publish or hand in (responsibility)

Before sending, signing or publishing something made with AI:

- **Read all of it.** Not just the beginning.
- **Check** facts, numbers and names (Discernment lesson).
- Ask yourself: *“Would I be comfortable defending every sentence of this text?”*

<Callout type="key" title="The golden rule">
  <p>AI is a collaborator, not a scapegoat. <strong>If you sign it, it’s yours</strong>: mistakes included.</p>
</Callout>

## Other risks to know about

- **AI scams**: cloned voices, fake photos, flawless emails. If a “relative” calls asking for urgent money, hang up and call back on the number you know.
- **Fake images and videos** (deepfakes): before sharing something shocking, look for confirmation from reliable sources.
- **Your relationship with AI**: it’s a great tool for venting or reflecting, but it doesn’t replace friends, family or a mental health professional.
